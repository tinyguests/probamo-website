Home / Drop-off audit

For products that lose users

Find out why users leave, and what to fix first

When people try a product and leave, there is usually a specific reason: a confusing step, a flow that fails on one device, or a promise the product doesn’t keep. A drop-off audit tests your product from your users’ point of view to find it.

Signs you need a drop-off audit

Sign-ups don’t become active users

People register or download, look around once, and never come back.

Trials don’t convert

Free trials end without an upgrade, and nobody can say exactly why.

Poor reviews and feedback

Ratings are lukewarm and complaints are vague: “confusing”, “didn’t work”, “not for me”.

Marketing spend isn’t paying off

Acquisition costs rise while retention stays flat.

How we find the cause

Expert walkthrough

We go through sign-up, onboarding, first use and upgrade as a new user would, on the devices your users have.

Testing with real users

We recruit people who match your audience and observe where they hesitate, get lost or give up.

Analytics and recordings

Where analytics or session recordings exist, we review them to confirm where users drop off.

Functional checks

Many drop-offs are bugs in disguise: a form that fails on one browser, an email that never arrives.

Promise versus product

We compare what your website and app store page promise with what the first session delivers.

Payment and upgrade path

Pricing pages, paywalls, checkout and the moment users are asked to pay.

Testing, not guesswork

Analytics show where users leave. They rarely show why. A drop-off audit combines the numbers with hands-on testing and observation of real people, so each drop-off point is linked to a concrete cause you can fix.

Because we test as well as review, we also catch the technical problems that look like a lack of interest: a broken step on one device, a slow screen, a confirmation that never arrives.

What a drop-off audit covers

Use one, two or all three methods: expert walkthrough, testing with real users, and review of your analytics and recordings. The scope and number of testing days are agreed before we start.

The result is one report: where users leave, why, the likely business impact, and a prioritised list of fixes, from quick wins to larger changes.

Common reasons users drop off

In our experience, most drop-offs come from a small number of causes: sign-up that asks for too much too early, onboarding that explains features instead of helping users reach a first result, steps that fail silently on certain devices, and pricing or upgrade screens that appear before users have seen the value.

Some causes are outside the product: a website or app store page that promises something the first session doesn’t deliver. A drop-off audit looks at the whole journey, from first visit to first payment, so the fix is aimed at the real cause.

What you get

Questions

What is a drop-off audit?

A drop-off audit finds out why users leave your product or never become paying customers. We test the product from your users’ point of view, combining an expert walkthrough, testing with real users and, where available, your analytics and session recordings.

Is a drop-off audit the same as UX testing?

It is a focused form of user experience testing. Instead of reviewing usability in general, it concentrates on one business question: where and why users stop, and what to fix first.

Do we need analytics for this?

No. Analytics and session recordings make the audit faster and more precise, but the walkthrough and testing with real users work without them.

How do you recruit test users?

We agree the profile with you, such as role, industry, experience or device, and recruit people who match it. Your own customers can also take part.

What types of product do you audit?

Web apps, SaaS products, mobile apps, webshops and customer portals: anything where users sign up, try, buy or subscribe.
