What we check

Agreed requirements

Contract, specification, user stories or emails: every agreed function is tested and marked as met, partly met or missing.

End-to-end journeys

Key processes from start to finish, the way customers and staff will really use them, including the unhappy paths.

Roles, permissions and data

Who can see and change what, and whether data is saved, calculated and displayed correctly.

Integrations

Payments, emails, notifications, exports and connections to the systems you already use.

Devices and browsers

The devices, browsers and screen sizes your users actually have.

What nobody wrote down

Obvious expectations missing from the specification: error messages, slow connections, edge cases.

Why independent acceptance testing?

Development teams test what they built, the way they built it. That testing is necessary, but it is not a neutral check against what you asked for, and it rarely covers the whole journey your customers and staff will take.

Your own team knows the product best, but testing properly takes days they usually don’t have. Independent acceptance testing fills that gap: an outside team with no stake in the outcome checks the software from the business side, against your requirements, your users and your risks.

Acceptance testing and UAT

User acceptance testing (UAT) is traditionally done by the people who will use the software. In practice, few businesses can spare them for long enough to test thoroughly. We run the acceptance test for you, using your requirements and real-world scenarios, and involve your users where their judgement matters most.

The result is a decision document, not just a bug list: what meets the agreement, what does not, what blocks sign-off, and what can wait.

Testing grounded in real operations

Our experience comes from operations-heavy industries: logistics, warehouse management, delivery services, e-commerce, micromobility, robotics and customer support. Software in these environments is used under time pressure, on shared devices and alongside other systems.

We test with that reality in mind. Can a dispatcher get through a shift? Can a customer complete an order on a slow phone? Will the support team be flooded on launch day? Those questions decide whether a release is really ready.

Questions

What is acceptance testing?

Acceptance testing verifies that new software or a release does what was agreed before you sign off, pay or go live. It is tested against requirements and real use, and ends with a clear recommendation: ready, ready with conditions, or not ready.

What is the difference between acceptance testing and UAT?

User acceptance testing (UAT) is acceptance testing carried out from the users’ point of view. We run it on your behalf, using your requirements and real-world scenarios, and involve your users where their input is needed.

Does it matter who built the software?

No. We test software built by in-house teams and by external partners in the same way: against what was agreed and how it will be used.

What if nothing was written down?

We help you capture the key expectations first, from contracts, emails, demos and your own knowledge of the business, so there is a clear basis for the test.

Will you work with our developers?

You decide. Reports go to you; with your approval we share findings directly with your development team and retest their fixes.

How long does an acceptance test take?

It depends on the size of the release. The number of testing days is agreed upfront and stated in the quote, planned around your sign-off or launch date.
